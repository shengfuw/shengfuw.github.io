About

I am a Ph.D. student in Sociology at New York University, supported by the Henry M. MacCracken Doctoral Fellowship. I study how social networks, culture, and cognition shape individual outcomes and collective behavior, using quantitative methods, causal inference, and computational tools. Before NYU, I received my M.A. and B.A. in Sociology from National Taiwan University (with a second field in Data Science and Social Inquiry) and was a visiting graduate student at the University of Wisconsin–Madison.

Research

Social networks & peer effects

Decomposing how peers shape academic achievement, and the mediating role of educational expectations (M.A. thesis, TSA Master's Thesis Award 2025).

Computational social science & LLMs

Evaluating the power and pitfalls of large language models for assessing survey questions (MAPOR 2024), and modeling the interaction between epidemics and behavior to explain oscillatory dynamics in pandemic response (TSA 2024).

Education & organizations

How curriculum reform reshapes students' college application preferences (TSA 2024), and the survival of college departments from an organizational ecology perspective (TSA 2021).
